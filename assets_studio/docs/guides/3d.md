# 3D: pipeline and prompts

<!-- markdownlint-disable MD013 -->

Twelve models made with the studio were rated 5 on its 0–5 scale (November
2026). All of them came out of the same four jobs with the same settings.
They include props (a scaffold platform, a kneeling stone statue, a
raven-skull charm, a dish of candles), buildings (a town hall, a spinning
cottage), characters and creatures (a hooded lantern-bearer, a ghoul, a
troll) and two sheep, in a dark painterly style and a bright hand-painted
one. Eleven reference prompts made them: the troll was rated 5 with two
textures. This guide records that job graph and what the prompts share.
Model comparisons and measurements: [image to 3D](../models/image-to-3d.md).

## Job graph

1. `qwen_image` / `text-to-image`: the reference image, a transparent PNG.
2. `hunyuan3d21` / `image-to-3d`, `image` = the reference: a coarse shape.
3. `hi3d` / `3d-refine`, `mesh` = the shape, `image` = the reference: the
   detailed surface, simplified to the game budget.
4. `trellis2_paint` / `3d-paint`, `mesh` = the refined mesh, `image` = the
   reference: a GLB with UVs and a PBR material.

The reference feeds all three mesh jobs. In the studio, the refine and paint
buttons pick the mesh and its source image automatically. In
[Flow](../../flow/README.md), import
[`flow/examples/3d-asset.json`](../../flow/examples/3d-asset.json): this graph
with one of the prompts below.

| Job | Settings | Time on an 8 GB RTX 3070 Laptop GPU |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `text-to-image` | `resolution` 1024x1024 (default 2048x2048) for every subject, full-body figures too; `transparent` on; 40 steps | 220–330 s per image |
| `image-to-3d` | The defaults: 50 steps, guidance 5, octree 384, `max_faces` 0 (0.6–1.6M triangles) | ~200 s |
| `3d-refine` | `resolution` 512 (one pass; the default 1024 makes two), `max_faces` 40000; 25 steps | 40–120 s |
| `3d-paint` | `texture_size` 1024 or 2048; 12 steps | 12–35 s |

- **Generation takes 8–12 minutes per model,** plus model loading. Submit
  several models at once: the launcher runs every ready job of the loaded
  model before switching, so they go stage by stage (all images, then all
  shapes and so on).
- **Check the reference before the mesh jobs.** It decides the result and
  is the slowest step. Some references were made two at a time (`count` 2)
  and the better one went on. A graph that runs straight through suits
  prompts already known to work.
- **Hi3D does the reduction.** Its `max_faces` sets the target of its own
  simplification of the full surface, so the mesh is simplified once. All
  twelve have 40K triangles, the budget the [character guide](characters.md)
  gives for a character. TRELLIS.2 Paint keeps the mesh, unwraps its UVs and
  paints it.
- **Texture size:** 1024 and 2048 both reached 5. The buildings and the
  scaffold used 2048, and the troll was rated 5 at both.
- **Colours:** the paint comes out darker than the reference and its hue
  drifts. Turn on `match_colours` when the asset must keep the reference's
  palette; see the [server notes](../../model_servers/trellis2_paint/README.md).

None of the twelve used these:

- `animation_tools` / `3d-retopo` reduces a painted mesh further. `decimate`
  keeps its UVs and material; `quadriflow` and `autoremesher` drop the paint.
- `3d-bake-normals` bakes a denser Hi3D result into a normal map for the
  painted mesh. With `max_faces` 0, Hi3D keeps about a million triangles.
- `3d-bake-colour` gives a coarser Hi3D result of the same shape the painted
  mesh's colours, for a LOD that matches LOD0 without a second paint.
- `paint21` (Hunyuan3D-Paint 2.1) paints from the source image instead of
  TRELLIS.2 Paint.

## The reference prompt

Describe one object by what it is made of, then end with what the whole set
shares: what the image is for, the view, the framing, the light, the style
and the prohibitions. The statue and the raven-skull charm, from the dark
painterly set, ended exactly like this:

> Game prop for a 3D model: *(object)*, three-quarter view from above, dark
> fantasy painterly style, even soft lighting, centered, isolated, no
> background, no shadow

The set's prompts began with “Game prop…”, “Game item…”, “Game creature
concept…” or “Game character concept for a 3D model:”, depending on the
object. Its other three varied the ending a little. The candles:

> Game item for a 3D model: three thick melted ivory wax candles of
> different heights standing together on a small round tarnished iron dish
> with three short legs, heavy wax drips running down, unlit black wicks,
> three-quarter view from slightly above, dark fantasy painterly style, even
> soft lighting, centered, isolated, no background, no shadow

The ghoul and the lantern-bearer added a palette and “clean readable
silhouette”:

> Game character concept for a 3D model: a gaunt hooded lantern-bearer in a
> long tattered charcoal-grey cloak with a deep hood shadowing the face,
> leather straps and small pouches, holding an ornate brass lantern with a
> warm glowing flame out to the side in the right hand, standing upright,
> full body visible from head to feet, three-quarter front view from
> slightly above, dark fantasy painterly style, muted desaturated palette
> with a warm amber lantern accent, even soft lighting, clean readable
> silhouette, centered, isolated, no background, no ground shadow

All four prompts of the bright hand-painted set put the object first and
ended the same way:

> a medieval half-timbered town hall with one tall square clock tower, red
> tiled roofs, a stone ground floor, a wooden notice board beside the arched
> door, on a low stone foundation. stylized 3D game asset render for an
> isometric fantasy village builder, hand-painted look, chunky simple
> readable shapes, warm saturated colors, soft even studio lighting,
> three-quarter view from slightly above, the entire object visible and
> centered, isolated on a plain background, no ground plane, no cast shadow,
> no text

The scaffold platform and the storybook-style sheep had endings of their own
with similar parts.

What the prompts share:

1. **One concrete object, described by its construction:** its parts,
   materials and colors, how they hold together, and counts. Examples:
   “thick old dark planks laid side by side, bound with rusted iron bands”,
   “three thick melted ivory wax candles of different heights”, “one tall
   square clock tower”. The phrases are separated by commas, but nearly every
   one describes the object. Bare tags (“3D character design, cartoon style,
   standing pose”) give generic results.
2. **What it stands on:** a plinth, a dish with three short legs, a low stone
   foundation, heavy crossed beams underneath. Characters and animals are
   “standing upright” or “standing on four legs”. With a base, the model
   stands on terrain without sinking or tipping.
3. **A three-quarter view from above,** in all eleven prompts, usually
   “from slightly above”. Hunyuan3D infers the hidden sides from the
   visible ones. A flat object needs a steeper view: “viewed from above at a
   steep three-quarter angle so the plank top is clearly visible” made the
   scaffold platform. Even the set for an isometric game asked for this view
   rather than an isometric one.
4. **The whole object in frame and centered:** “full body visible from head
   to feet”, “whole animal visible and centered”.
5. **Even, soft light without shadows:** “even soft lighting”, “no shadow”,
   “no ground shadow”. Shadows in the reference end up in the texture.
6. **Isolated, with `transparent` on:** “isolated, no background”, “no
   ground plane”.
7. **The style and palette in a few words, shared by the set, with colors
   named for the parts.** Examples: “dark fantasy painterly style, muted
   desaturated palette”, “hand-painted look, warm saturated colors”, one
   accent such as “a warm amber lantern accent” or “red string and a small
   bronze ring”. A shared palette without variation (“moss greens and cold
   greys”) once produced a nearly monochrome green world.
8. **Readable, solid shapes:** “compact shape”, “chunky simple readable
   shapes”, “clean readable silhouette”. Use thick parts rather than thin
   ones (“heavy crossed beams”, “a heavy wooden club”), and keep held things
   away from the body (the lantern “out to the side”).
9. **What the asset is for, in a phrase:** “Game prop for a 3D model”,
   “stylized 3D game asset render for an isometric fantasy village builder”,
   “dark fantasy video game level piece”.

Earlier rated models add two rules:

- **State game requirements as visible facts.** For a passage: “the opening
  is completely empty, wide enough for two people side by side, the
  background visible through it all the way down to the ground; no door, no
  bars, no floor slab across it”.
- **Add prohibitions for the object;** Qwen-Image follows them. For a cart:
  “no debris field, no barrels, no horse, no thin ropes”. For a log:
  “slightly hollow but not a pipe, no root fan”. General prohibitions go in
  the shared ending.

Characters to be rigged need a front view, an A-pose and empty hands instead:
see [characters](characters.md).

## What works poorly

- **Voids, fine gaps and cavities.** Arched roots with gaps beneath, or a
  sarcophagus defined by a dark opening under a shifted lid: Hunyuan3D fills
  them or tears the surface. Large openings work only when the background is
  visible through them.
- **Thin and porous structures.** Dense foliage, bushes, thin bars and
  branches become blobs. Better: “a few large rounded clumps”, “thick stone
  posts with iron bars”, dead trees with thick blunt branches. Hi3D gives
  thin branches thousands of non-manifold edges, which `3d-retopo`'s
  `decimate` cannot collapse.
- **Top-down views.** A ground tile seen from above becomes an upright plate,
  because the image is read as the shape's front. View it from about 30° and
  ask for “a thin slab of earth”.
- **An entire level as a diorama.** The output is diorama-scale with
  centimeter details, unsuitable for third-person navigation: levels averaged
  1.3 against 3.9 for enemies. Build levels from separate objects, and
  terrain from tiles or in an editor.
- **Composite objects in one phrase.** “A plank table on two barrels” became
  one barrel. Describe the construction (rule 1).
- **Sky.** Qwen-Image's equirectangular panoramas include ground in the
  lower half despite “no ground”. The horizon is central, so terrain must
  hide the bottom. The studio has no panorama or skybox mode.

## Transparent references

Generate the reference with `transparent` on. Qwen-Image decodes the alpha
itself, the 3D servers use it instead of removing the background, and
TRELLIS.2 Paint accepts only a cut-out PNG. Cut an opaque image (an upload or
an edit) out with BiRefNet first.

Hunyuan3D-2.1, Hi3D and Paint 2.1 remove an opaque background themselves with
rembg's `isnet-general-use`. The default `u2net` removed entire tree crowns
and silver sword blades, which no prompt could fix. Pale objects on white
(silver foliage, white stone, steel) remain vulnerable: if a 3D silhouette
loses a part, check the cutout first.

## Concept failures (results rated 0)

- **“Reference sheet”, “character sheet”, “turnaround”** produce multiple
  views, labels and detail panels. For 3D, ask for one figure: “full body
  single…”.
- **Vague prompts** (“3D character design, cartoon style, standing pose”)
  yield generic characters unrelated to the game's world. Specify identity,
  clothes and setting.
- **“Low-poly”, “in the style of a 2001 game”** yield grey or dark
  backgrounds and thin branches. Polygons in the image do not become mesh
  polygons.
- **Level dioramas:** Hunyuan3D reads 45° isometric views as fronts, so the
  top becomes a front wall and the play area is lost.
- **Thin flat objects seen end-on** (a sword blade from the front) are
  easily lost in background removal and mesh simplification.

## Detail scale

Image details suit the depicted object's size. A ground tile stretched to
18 m turns pebbles into boulders; flattening its height to 30% gives softer
terrain. Describe details for the size the object will have.

## Trees: deep roots rather than flat bases

A tree on a flat base (“standing on its own roots”, “flat base”) shows the
base on any slope. An untested alternative: extend the trunk below ground
into a deep, tapering mass of several thick roots, about a third of the
trunk's height, as though uprooted. Such a tree can be sunk into any terrain.
Prompt: “no flat base, no ground disc, no soil”.
